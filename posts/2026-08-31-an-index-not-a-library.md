I have wanted a site of my own for a while. Partly for essays, but mainly for a longer project: building a proper reference index for hydrogeology. The dream is simple to state — when a question comes up, you search here and find out, within seconds, *which book and which chapter* to open.

## Isn't this a waste of time?

I photographed part of my bookshelf — title pages and contents pages only, not full texts. There are too many books to scan whole.

A classmate told me this is wasted effort: better to organise papers instead, since papers can be downloaded and carry actual content.

I see it differently. This site was never meant to be a full-text library. It is an **index** — what librarians call a *finding aid*. It answers one question: "for this problem, which book, which chapter?" That is exactly the skill textbooks never teach, and exactly what beginners lack. Paper databases answer "what is the latest result"; textbooks and monographs answer "what is the framework". The two do not compete.

And an index is the one kind of project that welcomes being incomplete:

1. Start with titles and chapter skeletons — done;
2. Add chapter summaries, keywords and page numbers over time;
3. When a real problem sends me back to a chapter, the notes I take get attached to the entry;
4. Year by year, the index thickens into a genuine reference work.

I intend to do a PhD after this degree. This site will grow alongside that — slowly, and on purpose.

## What lives here

- **Essays** — professional and otherwise.
- **The Library** — a searchable chapter-level index. The first batch covers 26 English-language volumes, each with topics, a summary, "best for" guidance, a currency warning, and full-text search over the photographed contents pages.
- Later: reading notes, formula sheets, case digests. We'll see.

If you work on groundwater too, I hope this saves you a detour or two.
