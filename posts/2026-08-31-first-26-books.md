The Library's first batch comes from 26 English-language volumes on my shelf. The method: photograph each book's title page, imprint page and complete table of contents; run OCR over the photographs; then verify every record against library catalogues and publisher pages, and annotate it by hand.

## What each record contains

- **Bibliographic data** — title, authors, publisher, edition, ISBN, all cross-checked against the photographs and online catalogues;
- **Topics** — a tree of 17 subject headings, from groundwater fundamentals to geothermal and mine water;
- **Summary and "best for"** — what the book covers, and which questions it answers well;
- **A currency assessment** — most of these volumes date from 1970–1995. Each carries a currency-risk rating and a pointer to what should be consulted instead for anything regulatory, numerical or safety-critical. The old books earn their place through frameworks and classic methods, not current parameters;
- **A physical location** — box number and position, so a search result leads to the actual book;
- **Full contents text** — OCR of all 126 photographed contents pages, searchable by chapter title.

## Books worth meeting first

- **Groundwater** (Freeze & Cherry, 1979) — the first stop for the conceptual skeleton of the field; preserved open-access by the Groundwater Project as one of its defining textbooks.
- **Analysis and Evaluation of Pumping Test Data** (Kruseman & de Ridder) — the classic manual of analytical pumping-test methods: Theis, Thiem, Hantush and the rest; the fully revised 1990 edition is a free official PDF.
- **Construction Dewatering** (Powers, 1981) — among the most directly practical references on dewatering and groundwater control; a third edition appeared in 2007.
- **Field Hydrogeology** (Brassington) — a superbly organised field handbook; the series is now in its fifth edition (2023).
- **Study and Interpretation of the Chemical Characteristics of Natural Water** (Hem, USGS) — the classic on natural-water chemistry, free in the official USGS archive.

## Known gaps, on the record

The cataloguing left a to-do list: one contents page of *Geology and Radwaste* appears to have been missed; two box assignments were inferred from photograph order and await checking against the physical boxes. That is how this project works — put the skeleton up first, log what's missing, fill it in over time.
