My course ends on 29 September. The degree survives that date; the email address does not, and with it goes eligibility for every student programme in the industry. Most of these entitlements are one-directional — claim them now and you keep them afterwards, claim them late and you never had them.

I worked through the list in one evening. The useful part is not what is on offer, which any search will tell you, but where the applications break.

## Perpetual keys, via the Azure Education Hub

Sign in to the Azure portal with the university account, then Education → Software. What appears depends on your institution's subscription; mine had around sixty entries, of which the relevant ones were Windows 11 Education (the same key activates Windows 10 Education), Visio Professional, Project Professional, Access, Windows Server, Visual Studio Enterprise, and SQL Server with no key at all.

These keys do not expire and outlive the account that issued them. One per product per person, personal use, generally one machine each. Worth ten minutes if there is a new computer in your near future.

Not there, despite the reasonable expectation: perpetual Word, Excel and PowerPoint. Those reach students only through the Microsoft 365 subscription attached to the account, which degrades to read-only some weeks after it closes.

## GitHub Student Developer Pack

The most valuable item, and the only one that rejected me. Copilot, 180 Codespaces hours a month, private repositories on Pro, and partner credits.

The automated review came back within minutes with four faults, all of them pre-conditions rather than judgement calls:

1. Two-factor authentication enabled — then sign out and back in before reapplying.
2. Profile name filled in, in full. An empty field is an automatic reject.
3. Billing information carrying the same full name and an address. No payment method needed; the fields simply cannot be blank.
4. Proof captured with the device camera. The form offers an upload button too, and it does not satisfy the reviewer.

Two more things worth knowing before you start. Your university email has to be added and verified on the GitHub account first, or the school-matching step has nothing to work from. And the application dialog hangs on *gathering available school information* while it silently waits for browser permission to read your location and open your camera — grant both and it proceeds at once.

A physical student card held up to the webcam photographs far better than a letter on a phone screen, and carries the crest, student number, course and end date in one frame.

## JetBrains

The whole toolbox, free for a year, renewable while enrolled. Ten minutes end to end, no review queue, licence issued immediately.

The form asks you to verify a personal email alongside the university one. Do it — that address is what lets you sign in after the university address stops existing.

## Azure for Students

100 dollars of credit for twelve months, no card. Academic verification passed and the confirmation link registered, then the final step failed repeatedly with a generic retry message. Unresolved at the time of writing. Signing up with a personal Microsoft account rather than the university one should keep the subscription alive past graduation, though I cannot confirm that path completes.

## Two things to do anyway

Move anything you care about out of the university OneDrive. And keep your product keys somewhere that is not a university-hosted document.
