Hydrology in Practice

Covers the hydrological cycle, measurement of precipitation, evaporation, soil moisture, river flow, groundwater and water quality, then analytical methods for rainfall, evaporation, runoff, catchment modelling and stochastic hydrology, with engineering applications in flood routing, design floods, urban hydrology, water resources and river basin management.

Best for: learning the fundamentals of UK hydrological measurement, analysis and engineering design, especially rainfall, evaporation, river flow, groundwater, flood estimation and reservoir yield

Reading context

The basic measurement principles (rain gauges, current metering, dilution gauging, groundwater hydraulic testing) and analytical concepts (unit hydrograph, frequency analysis, flood routing) remain sound. However, UK flood-estimation practice has moved from the Flood Studies Report to the Flood Estimation Handbook, and meteorological data services, water quality standards and modelling packages are superseded; do not rely on this for current UK regulatory design floods, data sources or software.

Use Hydrology in Practice, 4th edition by Shaw, Beven, Chappell and Lamb (Spon Press/CRC, 2010) as the current replacement; the 1983 first edition is borrowable from Internet Archive.
