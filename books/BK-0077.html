Nuclear Well Logging in Hydrology

A 1971 IAEA working-group report on nuclear well logging for groundwater investigations, covering physical principles, equipment, safety and field procedures for natural-gamma, gamma-gamma, neutron and tracer methods, with interpretation for lithology, aquifer identification, porosity, moisture content, permeability, specific yield, fluid chemistry and flow.

Best for: the historical physical basis and interpretation of natural-gamma, gamma-gamma, neutron and tracer logs in groundwater investigations, not current instrumentation, calibration, digital data handling or radiation-safety practice

Reading context

The gamma-ray and neutron interaction physics remains valid, but the 1971 instruments, analogue data handling, calibration and especially radiation-safety and licensing practice are obsolete. Do not rely on this for current logging tool specifications, digital data acquisition or processing, or regulatory radiation protection; consult Keys (1990), IAEA TRS 91 (1983) and IAEA SSG-57.

No later edition; the subject was absorbed into the IAEA Guidebook on Nuclear Techniques in Hydrology (TRS 91, 1983), with Keys (1990) Borehole Geophysics Applied to Ground-Water Investigations and IAEA SSG-57 as modern replacements, and an archived IAEA PDF of TRS 126 is openly available via Internet Archive.
