Petrology of the Sedimentary Rocks

This classic descriptive text covers the origin, classification and petrography of sedimentary rocks, including rudaceous, arenaceous, argillaceous, carbonate, siliceous, ferruginous and carbonaceous deposits, with chapters on depositional environments, weathering, soils and diagenesis.

Best for: classic hand-specimen and thin-section identification of sedimentary rocks, especially sandstone, limestone and mudrock petrography

Reading context

The hand-specimen and thin-section descriptive criteria remain broadly valid for identifying sedimentary rock types, but the classification and terminology are partly superseded. Do not rely on this for modern diagenetic models, carbonate or siliciclastic classification schemes, depositional-environment interpretation, or current geological/regulatory standards; check against a modern sedimentary petrology text.

The 7th edition (Greensmith, Unwin Hyman, 1989; Springer reprint 2012) is the latest revision; for current practice, use a modern replacement such as Tucker, Sedimentary Petrology (Blackwell).
