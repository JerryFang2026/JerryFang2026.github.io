Elements of Structural Geology

Covers depositional and non-diastrophic structures, the physics of deformation with Mohr diagrams, planar and linear structures, joints, faults, folds, cleavage, major tectonic structures, igneous rocks, structural petrology (by E. den Tex), and morphotectonics.

Best for: hydrogeologists in fractured rock needing descriptive structural geology fundamentals: faults, folds, joints, cleavage and morphotectonics

Reading context

The descriptive terminology and geometric classification of faults, folds, joints and cleavage remain broadly valid, but the genetic and tectonic interpretations predate plate tectonics and modern strain analysis. Do not rely on this for current plate-tectonic settings, quantitative stress–strain analysis, or modern structural geology software and numerical methods.

Use Hills (1972) Elements of Structural Geology, 2nd ed. as the direct update, with Fossen (2016) Structural Geology, 2nd ed. as the current authoritative replacement; the 1963 Wiley printing is borrow-only on the Internet Archive.
