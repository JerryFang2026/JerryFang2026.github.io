Hydrology

Edited by Oscar E. Meinzer under the National Research Council’s Physics of the Earth series, this 1942 compendium covers precipitation, evaporation, snow and ice, glaciers, lakes, infiltration, transpiration, soil moisture, groundwater occurrence and movement, runoff, stream gauging, unit hydrographs, floods, and storage.

Best for: tracing the 1942 state of hydrology and the historical roots of modern groundwater concepts

Reading context

The descriptive conceptual chapters remain historically valuable, but every quantitative method, coefficient, and design standard has been superseded; groundwater flow equations and surface-water methods are presented in pre-computer forms. Do not rely on this for modern regulatory design, numerical modelling, or parameter estimation.

No later edition of this compendium exists; for the groundwater chapters use Freeze and Cherry (1979) Groundwater (freely available via the Groundwater Project), and for Meinzer’s groundwater definitions see USGS Water-Supply Paper 494.
